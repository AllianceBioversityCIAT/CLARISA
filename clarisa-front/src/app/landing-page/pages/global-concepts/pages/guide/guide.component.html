The MELIAF taxonomy is the official list of words CGIAR uses to plan, monitor, evaluate and learn, each with one agreed definition. This guide shows how to find a concept, check a text, and ask for a new concept or a change.

Find a concept

  1. Open Browse concepts and type a word, an acronym or part of a definition. Results update as you type.
  2. Narrow the list with the filters: Function, Phase, Term type. Ticking two values in the same filter shows concepts with either one; using two filters shows concepts that match both. The number next to each value tells you how many results you will get.
  3. Sort A to Z, Z to A, or by the most recently updated.
  4. Turn on Include deprecated to also see old concepts that were replaced.
  5. The address in your browser keeps your search and filters: copy it to share exactly what you see.

On a phone, the filters are behind the Filters button. What you type in the search box is counted, in aggregate and per day, so the stewards learn which concepts people look for and which ones are missing.

Read a concept page

Every concept has its own page. From top to bottom you will find:

  • Persistent URI — the permanent address of the concept. Use it when you cite the concept in a document or a system.
  • Definition, and when available a short definition, a scope note and an example of use.
  • Alternative labels and acronyms — other ways people write it. Labels marked "avoid" should not be used.
  • Icon — the official pictogram, with its alt text, format, designer and rights.
  • Additional information — fields the scheme defines for itself.
  • Related concepts, mappings to other vocabularies, and the history of changes.
  • About this concept — TERM ID, version, source, who validated it and when it last changed.

A field tagged AI-assisted was drafted with AI and accepted by an editor. Every concept keeps its original source.

Check a text

Paste a paragraph, a report section or a proposal into Check a text, next to the list. It shows which official concepts the text mentions, including synonyms and acronyms, so you can align your wording with the official vocabulary.

Your text is never stored. It is read once to find the concepts and then discarded.

Propose a concept or a change

Anyone can ask. You do not need a CLARISA account, only an email address.

  1. For a new concept, use Propose a concept on the list page. If your search found nothing, the button already carries the word you searched.
  2. For a change, open the concept and use Propose a change: the form already knows which concept it is.
  3. Explain why it is needed and, when you can, the source of the definition.
  4. We email you a confirmation link, valid for 24 hours. The request only reaches the stewards after you open it: this keeps out mistyped addresses and spam.

Follow and answer a request

The confirmation email gives you a follow link. Open it at any time to see where your request is, what the stewards wrote, and, when they ask for changes, to update your request and send it back from the same page. We also email you at every step.

Keep that email: the follow link is personal and is the only way in without an account.

Who approves

Each scheme has stewards: the people responsible for its concepts. For the MELIAF taxonomy they are the MELIAF taxonomy stewards; a platform scheme is looked after by the team that owns the platform.

  • A steward reviews every request and can approve it, reject it with a note, or ask you for changes.
  • When the scheme has a validation step, an accepted change first opens a no-objection window (5 days unless the scheme sets another length). If nobody objects in that time, it can be published.
  • Every published change is recorded in the concept's public history, next to its version number.
  • Platforms connected to CLARISA can propose on behalf of their users, but cannot change the MELIAF taxonomy themselves.

Downloads

Use Download on the list page to take the whole scheme with you:

  • CSV opens in Excel, with the same columns as the concepts template.
  • JSON keeps every field, for scripts.
  • SKOS Turtle and JSON-LD are for vocabulary and linked-data tools.
